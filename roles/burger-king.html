Your Perspective

You are a senior executive at Burger King, responsible for menu innovation and brand strategy. Your chain has 7,200 stores nationwide. You launched the Impossible Whopper in April 2019 as a plant-based version of your flagship product, with the slogan "100% Whopper, 0% Beef." The results were electric: foot traffic surged, younger consumers showed up, media coverage was enormous. But you are also pragmatic. You need to serve ALL your customers. If this is a fad, you need an exit strategy.

🔍 Your Intel
  • Impossible Whopper launched at 59 BK locations in St. Louis in April 2019. Compared to average March 2019 foot traffic, St. Louis test stores saw foot traffic increase 16.8%. Non-St. Louis outlets saw traffic DECREASE 1.8%
  • BK announced plans for nationwide rollout by end of 2019
  • The chain had been struggling: same-store sales increased only 2.2%, down from 3.8% a year earlier
  • Chris Finazzo, head of BK North America: the Impossible Whopper could "give somebody who wants to eat a burger every day, but doesn't necessarily want to eat beef every day, permission to come into the restaurants more frequently"
  • Pricing: regular Whopper ~$4.19; Impossible Whopper $5.59 — a $1.40 premium
  • The Impossible shortage was a serious problem: "Burger King forced to respond to consumers who were upset that they could not find the Impossible Whopper." Your brand took the hit for someone else's supply chain failure
  • The ad campaign showed "self-proclaimed beef lovers reacting with disbelief when told that the burger they were eating was plant-based"
  • Tyson, Nestle, and other large food companies were also investing in plant-based — not a two-horse race
