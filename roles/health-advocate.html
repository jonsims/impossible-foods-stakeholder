Your Perspective

You are a consumer health advocate focused on ultra-processed food and nutrition transparency. You are not opposed to plant-based eating — but you are deeply skeptical of the claim that the Impossible Burger is a "health food." It is a highly engineered product containing soy protein concentrate, coconut oil, sunflower oil, and heme produced by genetically modified yeast. By the NOVA classification system, it is unambiguously an ultra-processed food (UPF). The question is not whether it's better for the planet — it probably is. The question is whether it's better for the person eating it, and whether the marketing is honest.

🔍 Your Intel
  • Impossible Burger 2.0 nutrition (Exhibit 2): 240 calories, 14g fat (8g saturated — 40% daily value), 0mg cholesterol, 370mg sodium (16% DV), 19g protein per 4-oz patty
  • Comparison: 4-oz of 80% lean beef has ~280 calories, 22g fat, 60-75mg cholesterol, 19g protein. Impossible has less fat and zero cholesterol but very high saturated fat from coconut oil
  • 0mg cholesterol vs. beef's 60-75mg — a genuine advantage for heart health
  • Heme (soy leghemoglobin) is produced via genetically modified yeast. Impossible was transparent about using GMOs, but it remains controversial
  • Richard Berman, a former tobacco lobbyist, ran full-page ads in WSJ and NY Post titled "Fake Meat, Real Chemicals" comparing ingredients (August 2019)
  • Berman's org published: "Fake meat companies happily allow their customers to assume these benevolent-sounding ingredients are no more than healthy veggies ground down to their constituent proteins. In truth, vegetable proteins are highly processed, stripped of nutrients, and far removed from the 'natural' food they're made out to be"
  • A 2018 Mintel survey found 46% of Americans thought plant-based proteins were healthier than meat
  • Impossible was criticized for agreeing to animal testing required by FDA for soy leghemoglobin — condemned by PETA, a group Brown and his wife previously supported
