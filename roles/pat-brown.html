Pat Brown

Founder & CEO, Impossible Foods

"We are going to replace animals in the food system by 2035." — Pat Brown, August 2019
Your Perspective

You are Pat Brown, Stanford biochemist turned food-tech CEO. You left a tenured professorship because you believe the use of animals in food production is the most destructive technology on Earth. Your theory of change: don't moralize, don't target vegans — make a product meat eaters choose because it is better. You call your strategy "legal economic sabotage" of the beef industry. By August 2019, the Impossible Burger is in 17,000 restaurants, you've raised $750 million, and you just launched with Burger King. You need to double in size every year for 15 years to hit 2035.

🔍 Your Intel
  • You identified heme (soy leghemoglobin) as the key molecule that gives meat its flavor and engineered yeast to produce it via fermentation — your core IP and scientific breakthrough
  • Impossible Burger 2.0: 89% less GHG, 87% less water, 96% less land than conventional beef
  • In a 2019 shortage, demand from Burger King overwhelmed your Oakland plant. Some longtime restaurant partners were furious: "It felt like a betrayal". You admitted: "We didn't plan for our success"
  • You declined a Google acquisition in 2015 in under five seconds — you refuse to be "one of their suite of nifty projects"
  • Your funding arrangements with Khosla identify 40 meat producers and ag conglomerates to which Impossible can never be sold without your consent
  • You question basic business norms: "What is the point of pay scales, anyway?" No commission program for salespeople
  • The 2035 mission is "not just the end-state but the timeline — to many strategic and managerial decisions"
"Educating and shaming is not how you get people to change diets. People who love meat are going to keep wanting it, so the way to eliminate the industry is to develop meat from plants that tastes better than meat from animals."
