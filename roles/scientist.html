Your Perspective

You are an environmental scientist specializing in food system sustainability. The data is unambiguous: the livestock system is one of the most significant drivers of ecological degradation on the planet. It touches at least five of the nine planetary boundaries. The Impossible Burger's LCA numbers are genuinely impressive. But you are also a scientist — you know that a good product does not automatically mean a transformed system. U.S. plant-based meat is still less than 2% of total meat sales. The scale of the problem dwarfs the solution so far.

🔍 Your Intel
  • Livestock accounts for nearly 15% of global GHG emissions (FAO). Feed production/processing: 45%, enteric fermentation: 39%, manure: 10%
  • Beef cattle account for 41% of all livestock emissions. Beef has the highest emissions intensity per kg of protein
  • Beef requires 28x more land, 11x more irrigation water, 5x more GHG, and 6x more nitrogen fertilizer than the average of dairy/pork/poultry/eggs (PNAS)
  • About 40% of U.S. land area is used for livestock production. Beef = 88% of that land but only 7% of American calories
  • Impossible Burger 2.0: 89% less GHG, 87% less water, 96% less land vs. conventional beef
  • The livestock system affects at least 5 of 9 planetary boundaries: climate change, biosphere integrity, land-system change, biogeochemical flows (N/P), and freshwater use
  • Agriculture = ~70% of global freshwater use. Livestock production systems use 37-45% of global land surface
  • IPCC identified dietary change as "an important lever" for lessening livestock GHG (August 2019)
  • EAT-Lancet Commission (2019): "necessary shift" toward plant-based, defining "planetary health diet" with only 14g/day of beef, pork, lamb
  • From 1970 to 2012, wildlife populations shrank by an average of 58% (WWF)
